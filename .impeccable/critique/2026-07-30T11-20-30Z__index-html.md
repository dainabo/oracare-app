## Overall Impression

This looks like a finished product and behaves like a clickable mockup. The visual system is genuinely good - restrained palette, real type hierarchy, one hand-drawn illustration that could not be lifted into another app - and the domain writing is better than most shipped health products. Underneath, four of ten heuristics are at 0-1, sixteen controls are styled as live and do nothing, seven medical questions arrive pre-answered on the user's behalf, and there is no error state anywhere in the app.

The single biggest opportunity is not visual. It is that the seven-question assessment currently fabricates patient-reported data and then builds a clinical plan and a health score on it. Everything else on this list is smaller than that.

## What's Working

1. **The "why we're asking" copy on all seven assessment steps** (index.html:176, 203, 264, 288, 315, 339, 363). Each question is paired with one sentence of clinical rationale - "The ADA recommends two dental cleanings during pregnancy. Regular check-ups are safe and important." 2. **The Capture screen's composition and interaction model** (index.html:711-828). ## Priority Issues

**[P0] All seven assessment questions arrive pre-answered with clinical claims, and "None of these apply" does not clear them**

Why it matters: index.html:183, 205, 221, 272, 293, 320, 347, 368 all ship with selected/checked. A user who taps Continue seven times has told the app she has bleeding gums and dry mouth, rarely flosses, has not seen a dentist in 3-6 months, and vomits occasionally. The app then builds a "personalized" clinical plan and a 72 health score on that. There is no back between steps and no review screen, so she can never see or correct what she supposedly said. Tapping "None of these apply" (index.html:256) advances without clearing anything. This is not a defaulting convention; it is fabricated patient-reported data driving clinical output.

Fix: Strip every selected/checked from initial markup. Add a disabled state to .btn-primary (none exists) and gate Continue on a selection per step. Make "None of these apply" call a clearSymptoms() that unchecks all six, then advances. Add a "Review your answers" step before "Build My Care Plan" with tap-to-edit rows. Let goAssessStep move backwards for steps 2-7.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the care plan cannot be completed, why is it called a plan? What does that screen look like if its only job is to tell her the one thing to do today, with the other six behind "the rest of your plan"?
2. Salmon is both your brand's warmth and your danger signal. If inflammation got its own alarm hue and salmon went back to being the brand, what would Results feel like - and would you still need the "Attention Needed" chip?
3. Three flowers and a blonde pregnant-woman emoji currently carry "hormonal life stage". Your logo is already an arch that reads as a gum line and a protective curve. What do five stages look like as five variations of that one drawn form - and does the emoji layer survive that exercise anywhere in the app?
4. If all seven questions arrived blank, would the demo still feel fast enough? If not, the flow's perceived speed is coming from the pre-filled answers rather than from the design - worth knowing before this goes in front of anyone as a health product.
